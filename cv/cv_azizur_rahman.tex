% ── Research Profile ──────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Research Profile}

Computer Science researcher with an M.S.\ in Computer Science specialising in
Artificial Intelligence (GPA: 3.97\,/\,4.00), with research experience spanning
deep learning, convolutional neural networks, computer vision, medical imaging,
Transformer architectures, transfer learning, multimodal learning, and explainable
AI\@. My work focuses on developing robust, efficient, and interpretable deep
learning architectures for visual recognition and medical image analysis, with
particular interest in CNN-based representation learning, CNN–Transformer hybrid
models, transfer learning, and uncertainty-aware prediction. Application domains
include gastrointestinal endoscopy risk stratification, Parkinson's disease
detection, agricultural disease recognition, and housing affordability forecasting.
My long-term research direction centres on building efficient, interpretable, and
uncertainty-aware deep learning systems that are reliable enough for high-stakes
real-world deployment — particularly in medical imaging and clinical decision support.

\pubentry
  {Beyond Binary: Four-Class Risk Stratification from Gastrointestinal Endoscopy
   Using Asymmetric-Cost Lightweight CNN–Transformer Learning}
  {\textbf{Azizur Rahman}, Nakib Uddin Ahmed, Mehjabin Ferdous, Kadirur Rahman Chowdhury}
  {Medical \& Biological Engineering \& Computing (MBEC), Springer, 2026}
  {Under Review}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Developed a four-class gastrointestinal lesion risk stratification framework
        using lightweight CNN and Transformer architectures (DenseNet-121, EfficientNet-B0,
        DeiT-Tiny).
  \item Mapped 21 of 23 HyperKvasir finding classes into four clinically actionable risk
        tiers; achieved macro F1\,=\,0.84 on HyperKvasir and 0.78 zero-shot on Kvasir-v2.
  \item Proposed an Asymmetric Endoscopy Loss (AEL) with a 5$\times$ high-risk penalty;
        applied MC Dropout ($T$\,=\,30, $\tau$\,=\,0.75) for uncertainty-aware clinical
        referral decisions.
  \item Automated triage of 44.9\% low-confidence cases with zero missed high-risk lesions.
\end{itemize}
